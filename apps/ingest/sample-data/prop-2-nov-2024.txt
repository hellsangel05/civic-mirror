CALIFORNIA — NOVEMBER 5, 2024 GENERAL ELECTION
PROPOSITION 2
PUBLIC EDUCATION FACILITIES BOND MEASURE

===== BALLOT QUESTION =====
Shall the state authorize $10 billion in general obligation bonds for repair, upgrade, and construction of facilities at K-12 public schools (including charter schools) and community colleges, including for improvement of health and safety conditions and classroom upgrades? Authorizes $8.5 billion for K-12 school facilities and $1.5 billion for community college facilities.

===== IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS (Legislative Analyst, excerpted) =====
Proposition 2 would authorize the state to sell $10 billion in general obligation bonds for public education facilities. Of the total, $8.5 billion would be available for K-12 public school facilities and $1.5 billion for community college facilities. Bond funds could be used for new construction, modernization, career technical education facilities, and charter school facilities. The state would generally cover between 50 and 55 percent of construction project costs and 60 to 65 percent of modernization project costs, with local districts covering the remainder through local bonds or other funding.

The measure also removes the requirement that school districts contribute a specified local match when receiving state bond funds for career technical education projects.

===== FISCAL IMPACT STATEMENT =====
Increased state costs of about $500 million annually for approximately 35 years to repay the bond. The total cost to taxpayers, including principal and interest, is estimated at approximately $18 billion over the repayment period. Bond repayment comes from the state General Fund.

===== ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF PROPOSITION 2 =====
California's school buildings are aging and in many cases dangerously outdated. At least 38 percent of the state's K-12 students attend schools that do not meet minimum safety standards. Many school facilities exceed 50 years old and suffer from dry rot, mold, malfunctioning electrical systems, gas leaks, and other health and safety hazards.

Proposition 2 will help fix these issues and ensure that every student in the state has a safe and up-to-date place to learn. It also funds teacher and staff housing to help address California's housing shortage and teacher recruitment challenges. The measure includes career technical education facilities to train students for good-paying jobs.

Signed by: Tony Thurmond, State Superintendent of Public Instruction; Fiona Ma, State Treasurer

===== ARGUMENT AGAINST PROPOSITION 2 =====
Taxpayers already give ample support to schools, and the state should pay for school repairs out of the existing general fund budget rather than taking on $10 billion in additional debt that will cost $18 billion when repaid with interest.

It is reckless to borrow billions more to pay for more school buildings when district enrollment is declining. California has experienced the 6th consecutive decrease in total public K-12 enrollment. There is no accountability mechanism in this bond to ensure the money is spent wisely.

Signed by: Jon Coupal, President, Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association; Bill Essayli, Assemblymember
