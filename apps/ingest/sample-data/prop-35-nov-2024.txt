CALIFORNIA — NOVEMBER 5, 2024 GENERAL ELECTION
PROPOSITION 35
MANAGED CARE ORGANIZATION TAX AUTHORIZATION — PERMANENT FUNDING FOR MEDI-CAL HEALTH CARE SERVICES — INITIATIVE STATUTE

===== BALLOT QUESTION =====
Shall this initiative statute make permanent the existing tax on managed health care insurance plans, which currently expires in 2026, and require that revenue from the tax be used to fund Medi-Cal health care services, including increased payments to doctors, clinics, hospitals, and other providers that treat Medi-Cal patients, while prohibiting the legislature from using these revenues for other budget purposes?

===== IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS (Legislative Analyst, excerpted) =====
California currently imposes a tax on managed care organizations (health insurance plans). The tax was originally temporary and scheduled to expire on January 1, 2027. Revenue from this tax is used to draw additional federal matching funds for the state's Medi-Cal program, which provides healthcare to low-income residents and people with disabilities.

Proposition 35 would make this tax permanent and would require that the revenue be spent specifically on Medi-Cal services. The measure would restrict the legislature from diverting these funds to other parts of the budget. Specific spending priorities include: increased payment rates for Medi-Cal providers; expanded access to specialty care, mental health services, and family planning; workforce training; and health information technology.

===== FISCAL IMPACT STATEMENT =====
The existing tax generates approximately $5 billion annually. Making it permanent ensures ongoing revenue for Medi-Cal. However, the state Department of Finance warns that restricting how these funds are used could cost the state general fund approximately $12 billion over the next three years, because the legislature currently uses some of this revenue to cover general Medi-Cal expenses that would need to be funded from other sources.

The measure could also affect federal funding if changes to the tax structure do not receive federal approval. The net impact depends on complex interactions between state spending, federal matching funds, and legislative budget decisions.

===== ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF PROPOSITION 35 =====
Medi-Cal provides healthcare to over 15 million Californians — more than a third of the state's population. But Medi-Cal pays doctors and hospitals so little that many refuse to accept Medi-Cal patients, creating long wait times and limited access to care.

Proposition 35 ensures that the managed care tax revenue goes where it's supposed to — paying doctors and hospitals that serve low-income patients. It prevents politicians from raiding healthcare dollars to plug other budget holes. Nearly the entire healthcare industry supports this measure because they see firsthand how underpayment hurts patients.

Signed by: Planned Parenthood Affiliates of California; American College of Obstetricians & Gynecologists; American Academy of Pediatrics; California Medical Association

===== ARGUMENT AGAINST PROPOSITION 35 =====
We agree that Medi-Cal providers need to be paid more. But Proposition 35 could backfire and cause California to lose billions in federal funding. The measure locks in spending requirements that may conflict with federal rules, and if the federal government does not approve the tax structure, the state could lose matching funds.

The proposition also ties the legislature's hands during budget crises. If the state faces an emergency — like a pandemic or natural disaster — lawmakers would be unable to use these funds flexibly. Good intentions don't guarantee good policy.

Signed by: California Pan-Ethnic Health Network; The Children's Partnership; Western Center on Law and Poverty; Disability Rights California

===== LISTED SUPPORTERS =====
Planned Parenthood Affiliates of California; American College of Obstetricians & Gynecologists; American Academy of Pediatrics; California Medical Association; California Hospital Association; California Dental Association; California Primary Care Association; nearly the entire healthcare industry.

===== LISTED OPPONENTS =====
California Pan-Ethnic Health Network; The Children's Partnership; Western Center on Law and Poverty; Disability Rights California; some community health advocates and seniors groups.

===== FUNDING (approximate, as of November 2024) =====
Support committees: approximately $50 million raised from hospitals, doctors groups, insurers, and other healthcare industry organizations.
Opposition committees: no significant formal campaign spending was raised against this measure.

===== ELECTION RESULT =====
PASSED: Approved by a significant margin (called early on election night).
