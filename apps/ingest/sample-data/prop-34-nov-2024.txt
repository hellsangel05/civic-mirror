CALIFORNIA — NOVEMBER 5, 2024 GENERAL ELECTION
PROPOSITION 34
REQUIRE CERTAIN PARTICIPANTS IN MEDI-CAL RX PROGRAM TO SPEND 98% OF REVENUES ON PATIENT CARE — INITIATIVE STATUTE

===== BALLOT QUESTION =====
Shall this initiative statute require certain healthcare providers that participate in the federal 340B drug discount program and that spend more than $100 million on expenses other than direct patient care, operate multifamily housing with 500 or more severe health and safety violations, to spend at least 98% of net revenues from the 340B program on direct patient care?

===== IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS (Legislative Analyst, excerpted) =====
The federal 340B Drug Pricing Program allows eligible healthcare providers to purchase prescription drugs at significantly reduced prices. Some providers use the difference between the purchase price and what they charge patients or insurers to fund other activities, including non-patient-care activities.

Proposition 34 would require certain 340B providers to spend at least 98 percent of revenues from the program on direct patient care. The measure applies only to entities that: (1) spend more than $100 million on expenses other than direct patient care, (2) own and operate apartment buildings, and (3) have accumulated at least 500 severe health and safety violations in the past decade. As far as can be determined, only one organization currently meets all three criteria: the AIDS Healthcare Foundation.

The measure would also permanently authorize the state to negotiate drug prices through the Medi-Cal Rx program and require the state to join the federal 340B program for this purpose.

===== FISCAL IMPACT STATEMENT =====
The direct fiscal impact on state and local governments is likely minimal. The primary impact would be on the affected healthcare provider(s), which would be required to redirect revenues toward direct patient care. The Medi-Cal Rx authorization provisions could potentially generate state savings on prescription drug costs, though the amount is uncertain.

===== ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF PROPOSITION 34 =====
Money from discounted prescription drugs should go to treating patients, not to political campaigns and other non-healthcare spending. The 340B program exists to help vulnerable patients get affordable medication. Proposition 34 simply ensures that the money stays in patient care.

Healthcare organizations that receive these deep discounts have a responsibility to their patients. It's common sense: if you receive special pricing to serve low-income patients, spend that money on serving low-income patients.

Signed by: California Chronic Care Coalition; ALS Association; Defeating Epilepsy Foundation; California Senior Alliance

===== ARGUMENT AGAINST PROPOSITION 34 =====
Proposition 34 is a political hit job paid for by the apartment landlord lobby to silence the AIDS Healthcare Foundation, which has spent millions advocating for rent control (Proposition 33). In a democracy, campaigns should make their case to voters, not use the ballot to silence their opponents.

This measure is written so narrowly that it only applies to one organization. Both the U.S. and state constitutions prohibit laws that single out a specific person or organization for punishment. This measure is likely illegal and unenforceable. It sets a dangerous precedent of weaponizing the initiative process for political revenge.

Signed by: Michael Weinstein, President, AIDS Healthcare Foundation; Consumer Watchdog; National Organization for Women
