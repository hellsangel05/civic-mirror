CALIFORNIA — NOVEMBER 5, 2024 GENERAL ELECTION
PROPOSITION 32
RAISES MINIMUM WAGE — INITIATIVE STATUTE (THE LIVING WAGE ACT)

===== BALLOT QUESTION =====
Shall this initiative statute raise the minimum wage for all workers? For employers with 26 or more employees, the minimum wage would increase to $17 per hour upon effective date and to $18 per hour on January 1, 2025. For employers with 25 or fewer employees, the minimum wage would increase to $17 per hour on January 1, 2025, and to $18 per hour on January 1, 2026. Beginning in 2027, annual minimum wage increases would be adjusted for inflation up to a maximum of 3.5%.

===== IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS (Legislative Analyst, excerpted) =====
Under current law, California's minimum wage is $16 per hour for all workers, with annual adjustments based on inflation. Some sectors already have higher minimums: fast-food workers receive $20 per hour, and healthcare workers are phasing toward $25 per hour.

Proposition 32 would raise the statewide minimum wage to $18 per hour through a phased schedule. For large employers (26+ employees), the increase to $17 takes effect immediately and $18 begins January 1, 2025. For small employers (25 or fewer employees), the increase to $17 begins January 1, 2025, and $18 begins January 1, 2026. Starting in 2027, the minimum wage would increase annually by the rate of inflation, capped at 3.5 percent per year. The governor could temporarily pause increases in the event of a budget deficit or economic downturn.

===== FISCAL IMPACT STATEMENT =====
The measure would increase wages for an estimated 2 million California workers. The state and local governments would face increased costs for government employees and contractors paid at or near the minimum wage. The state could also see increased income tax revenue from higher earnings and potentially increased costs for public assistance programs. The overall fiscal impact is uncertain but likely modest relative to total state revenues.

===== ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF PROPOSITION 32 =====
No one working full-time should live in poverty. California's cost of living has skyrocketed, but the minimum wage hasn't kept pace. At $16 per hour, a full-time worker earns only about $33,000 per year — far below what's needed to afford rent, food, and basic necessities in most of the state.

Proposition 32 gives 2 million hardworking Californians a raise they deserve. It phases in gradually, giving businesses time to adjust. And it includes a safety valve: the governor can pause increases during economic downturns. This is a fair, responsible way to help working families.

Signed by: Joe Sanberg, Investor and Anti-Poverty Advocate

===== ARGUMENT AGAINST PROPOSITION 32 =====
Higher minimum wages lead to higher prices for consumers and job losses for the very workers they claim to help. When the city of West Hollywood implemented a local minimum wage of $19.08 per hour, 42 percent of responding businesses reported laying off workers or reducing hours.

Markets, not politicians and bureaucrats, ought to be dictating the financial growth and success of working men and women in California. California already has one of the highest minimum wages in the nation. Adding more costs on small businesses will force closures and accelerate the exodus of businesses from the state.

Signed by: John Kabateck, State Director, National Federation of Independent Business; Tom Scott, Executive Vice President, California Employers Association

===== FUNDING (approximate, as of November 2024) =====
Support committees: approximately $948,000 raised. Kevin de León raised more than $800,000. Joe Sanberg invested approximately $10 million during the 2022 cycle to qualify the measure for the ballot.
Opposition committees: approximately $855,000 raised. Largest donor: Western Growers Issues PAC ($250,000); California Growers Association and California Chamber of Commerce ($15,000 each); California Retailers Association, NFIB, and Western Growers Service Corporation ($10,000 each).

===== ELECTION RESULT =====
FAILED: 49.3% Yes, 50.7% No (extremely close margin).
