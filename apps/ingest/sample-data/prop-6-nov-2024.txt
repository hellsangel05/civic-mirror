CALIFORNIA — NOVEMBER 5, 2024 GENERAL ELECTION
PROPOSITION 6
REMOVE INVOLUNTARY SERVITUDE AS PUNISHMENT FOR CRIME AMENDMENT

===== BALLOT QUESTION =====
Shall the California Constitution be amended to remove the current provision that allows involuntary servitude (forced labor) as a punishment for crime, and to prohibit state prisons from disciplining incarcerated persons who refuse a work assignment?

===== IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS (Legislative Analyst, excerpted) =====
The California Constitution currently prohibits involuntary servitude but includes an exception: forced labor may be imposed as punishment for a crime. Proposition 6 would remove this exception and add language explicitly prohibiting the state from disciplining incarcerated persons for refusing to work.

The measure does not eliminate prison work programs. Prisons could still offer work opportunities and provide incentives for voluntary participation, such as sentence credits. However, incarcerated persons could no longer be punished (through disciplinary action or loss of privileges) for declining work assignments.

===== FISCAL IMPACT STATEMENT =====
The fiscal impact is uncertain but potentially significant. If fewer incarcerated persons choose to work, the state could face increased operational costs for prison maintenance and services currently performed by incarcerated workers. The California Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation estimates it could need to hire additional staff or contract services. However, the measure could also reduce costs associated with disciplinary proceedings. Overall, the net fiscal impact could range from minimal to tens of millions of dollars annually.

===== ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF PROPOSITION 6 =====
Proposition 6 ends slavery in California. Involuntary servitude — forced labor — is one of the last remnants of slavery in our state constitution. The 13th Amendment to the U.S. Constitution abolished slavery but included an exception for punishment of crime, and California adopted similar language. It is past time to remove this stain from our constitution.

This measure replaces coerced labor with voluntary work programs. Research shows that voluntary participation leads to better rehabilitation outcomes and lower recidivism. This is about human dignity, rehabilitation, and aligning California's constitution with our values.

Signed by: Lashrecse Aird, Senator; Members of the California Black Legislative Caucus

===== ARGUMENT AGAINST PROPOSITION 6 =====
No argument against Proposition 6 was submitted to the official voter guide.

However, critics have expressed concerns that eliminating the ability to require work could disrupt prison operations and increase costs. Some argue that structured work programs teach valuable skills and provide meaningful activity during incarceration, and that removing consequences for refusal could undermine these programs.
