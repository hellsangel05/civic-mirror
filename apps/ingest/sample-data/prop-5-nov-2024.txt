CALIFORNIA — NOVEMBER 5, 2024 GENERAL ELECTION
PROPOSITION 5
LOWER SUPERMAJORITY REQUIREMENT TO 55% FOR LOCAL BOND MEASURES TO FUND HOUSING AND PUBLIC INFRASTRUCTURE

===== BALLOT QUESTION =====
Shall the California Constitution be amended to allow local bonds for affordable housing and public infrastructure to be approved with 55% voter approval (rather than the current two-thirds requirement), and repaid through an increase in property taxes?

===== IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS (Legislative Analyst, excerpted) =====
Under current law, local governments generally need approval from two-thirds of voters to issue bonds that are repaid through higher property taxes. Proposition 5 would lower this threshold to 55 percent for bonds used to fund affordable housing and public infrastructure, including transit, streets, water, parks, and other community facilities.

The measure does not apply to bonds for single-family home purchases. It would apply to general-purpose local governments (cities, counties, special districts) but would not change the existing 55 percent threshold that already applies to school and community college bonds.

===== FISCAL IMPACT STATEMENT =====
The measure would potentially increase local borrowing capacity because bonds would be easier to pass at 55 percent than two-thirds. This could lead to higher local property tax rates in communities where voters approve new bonds. The fiscal impact depends on how many additional bonds are proposed and passed under the lower threshold. There is no direct state fiscal impact.

===== ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF PROPOSITION 5 =====
California faces a severe housing and infrastructure crisis. The current two-thirds requirement makes it nearly impossible for local communities to fund the affordable housing, transit improvements, and public facilities they desperately need. A 55 percent threshold is the same standard that already applies to school bonds and is a strong supermajority that ensures broad community support.

Proposition 5 empowers local communities to make their own decisions about investing in affordable housing and critical infrastructure. It gives residents the ability to address homelessness and improve their neighborhoods.

Signed by: Bob Hertzberg, former State Senator

===== ARGUMENT AGAINST PROPOSITION 5 =====
Proposition 5 makes it easier for local governments to raise your property taxes. The two-thirds requirement exists to protect homeowners and renters from excessive taxation. Lowering the threshold will lead to a flood of new bond measures and higher property taxes.

Higher property taxes get passed on to renters through higher rents, making the housing crisis worse, not better. Local governments should find ways to fund housing and infrastructure within existing budgets rather than raising taxes.

Signed by: Jon Coupal, President, Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association

===== ELECTION RESULT =====
FAILED: Did not reach the required simple majority. Approximately 45% Yes, 55% No.
