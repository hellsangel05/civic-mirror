CALIFORNIA — NOVEMBER 5, 2024 GENERAL ELECTION
PROPOSITION 3
CONSTITUTIONAL RIGHT TO MARRY

===== BALLOT QUESTION =====
Shall the California Constitution be amended to remove the provision that "only marriage between a man and a woman is valid or recognized in California" and instead provide that "the right to marry is a fundamental right"?

===== IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS (Legislative Analyst, excerpted) =====
In 2008, California voters approved Proposition 8, which added language to the state constitution stating that "only marriage between a man and a woman is valid or recognized in California." In 2013, the U.S. Supreme Court effectively invalidated Proposition 8 in Hollingsworth v. Perry, and in 2015, the Supreme Court's decision in Obergefell v. Hodges established a constitutional right to same-sex marriage nationwide. Although these rulings mean that Proposition 8's language is not currently enforceable, it still remains in the California Constitution.

Proposition 3 would remove the Proposition 8 language and replace it with a statement that "the right to marry is a fundamental right." This is intended to protect marriage equality in California's constitution regardless of future changes to federal law.

===== FISCAL IMPACT STATEMENT =====
The measure would have no direct fiscal effect on state or local governments.

===== ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF PROPOSITION 3 =====
Proposition 3 removes discriminatory, outdated language from the California Constitution. Even though same-sex marriage is currently legal under federal law, the Proposition 8 language is a stain on our constitution that should be removed. If the U.S. Supreme Court were ever to reverse its position on marriage equality, Proposition 3 ensures California law would continue to protect the right to marry for all couples.

This is about dignity and equal treatment under the law. Removing this language sends a clear signal that California values all families.

Signed by: Toni G. Atkins, President pro Tempore of the Senate; Scott Wiener, Senator

===== ARGUMENT AGAINST PROPOSITION 3 =====
Opponents argued that the broad language "the right to marry is a fundamental right" removes all protections on marriage, including limits on children, close relatives, and three or more people marrying each other. They contend that existing law already protects same-sex marriage and this proposition is unnecessary and could have unintended consequences.

Signed by: Jonathan Keller, President, California Family Council

===== LISTED SUPPORTERS =====

===== LISTED OPPONENTS =====
