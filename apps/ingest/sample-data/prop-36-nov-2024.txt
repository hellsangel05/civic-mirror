CALIFORNIA — NOVEMBER 5, 2024 GENERAL ELECTION
PROPOSITION 36
DRUG AND THEFT CRIME PENALTIES AND TREATMENT-MANDATED FELONIES — INITIATIVE STATUTE

===== BALLOT QUESTION =====
Shall this initiative statute allow felony charges and increased sentences for certain drug and theft crimes, including for possessing certain drugs and for thefts under $950 if the defendant has two prior theft convictions, and create a new category of "treatment-mandated felony" for certain drug possession offenses, where charges would be dismissed upon completion of treatment?

===== IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS (Legislative Analyst, excerpted) =====
In 2014, California voters approved Proposition 47, which reclassified certain nonviolent drug and property crimes from felonies to misdemeanors. Under Proposition 47, theft of property worth $950 or less is a misdemeanor, and simple possession of most illegal drugs is a misdemeanor.

Proposition 36 would partially roll back these changes. Specifically, it would: (1) Allow prosecutors to charge theft under $950 as a felony if the defendant has two or more prior theft convictions; (2) Allow felony charges for possessing certain drugs, including fentanyl, when combined with a loaded firearm; (3) Create a new "treatment-mandated felony" category for certain repeat drug possession offenses — defendants who complete a court-ordered treatment program would have their felony charges dismissed, while those who fail or refuse treatment could face up to three years in state prison; (4) Require sentences for certain theft crimes to be served in state prison rather than county jail.

===== FISCAL IMPACT STATEMENT =====
Increased state and local costs for law enforcement, courts, and incarceration. State prison costs could increase by tens of millions to potentially over $100 million annually as more offenders are sentenced to state prison rather than county jail. County jail costs could decrease somewhat as some offenders shift to state facilities. There would also be increased costs for court-ordered treatment programs, partially offset by savings if treatment reduces recidivism. The net fiscal impact is uncertain but likely results in increased overall criminal justice costs.

===== ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF PROPOSITION 36 =====
Proposition 47 went too far. It effectively decriminalized retail theft and drug crimes, leading to a surge in smash-and-grab robberies, organized retail theft, and open-air drug markets. Communities across California have been devastated.

Proposition 36 takes a balanced approach: it increases penalties for repeat theft offenders while offering drug treatment as an alternative to prison for people struggling with addiction. The treatment-mandated felony is a carrot-and-stick approach — complete treatment and the felony goes away; refuse treatment and face consequences.

Signed by: Sacramento County District Attorney Thien Ho; San Diego Mayor Todd Gloria; San Francisco Mayor London Breed (D); California District Attorneys Association; California State Sheriffs' Association

===== ARGUMENT AGAINST PROPOSITION 36 =====
Proposition 36 takes California backward. It will fill prisons, not solve problems. Studies show that longer prison sentences do not reduce drug use or theft. What works is investment in treatment, mental health services, and supportive housing — not mass incarceration.

The measure will cost taxpayers hundreds of millions of dollars in prison spending. It disproportionately harms communities of color and people experiencing homelessness and mental illness. We should invest in proven solutions, not repeat the failed tough-on-crime policies of the past.

Signed by: Californians United to Oppose Proposition 36; ACLU of Northern California; Anti-Recidivism Coalition; League of Women Voters of California
