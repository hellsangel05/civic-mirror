CALIFORNIA — NOVEMBER 5, 2024 GENERAL ELECTION
PROPOSITION 33
PROHIBIT STATE LIMITATIONS ON LOCAL RENT CONTROL — INITIATIVE STATUTE

===== BALLOT QUESTION =====
Shall this initiative statute repeal the Costa-Hawkins Rental Housing Act of 1995, thereby allowing local governments to limit the rental rates that landlords may charge for residential properties, including single-family homes and properties first occupied after February 1, 1995, and to restrict landlords from resetting rent to market rate when a tenant moves out?

===== IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS (Legislative Analyst, excerpted) =====
California's Costa-Hawkins Rental Housing Act (1995) limits the types of rent control that local governments can impose. Under current law, local governments cannot limit rents on single-family homes, condominiums, or housing built after February 1, 1995. Additionally, when a tenant moves out of a rent-controlled unit, the landlord may reset the rent to market rate (vacancy decontrol).

Proposition 33 would repeal the Costa-Hawkins Act entirely. This would allow cities and counties to pass rent control ordinances that apply to any type of residential property, regardless of when it was built or the type of housing. Local governments could also prevent landlords from resetting rent to market rate between tenancies.

The actual impact depends on what rent control laws cities and counties choose to enact if the measure passes.

===== FISCAL IMPACT STATEMENT =====
The fiscal impact is uncertain and depends on the rent control laws local governments enact. Potential impacts include: reduced property tax revenues if rent control limits property value growth; reduced income for landlords, potentially affecting state income tax revenues; possible impacts on housing construction if developers perceive reduced returns. Overall, the effect could range from a minor decrease to a significant reduction in state and local tax revenues.

===== ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF PROPOSITION 33 =====
California's housing crisis is devastating working families, seniors, and communities of color. Rents are out of control, and existing state law ties the hands of local communities that want to protect their residents. Proposition 33 gives cities and counties the power to pass common-sense rent control laws that work for their communities.

No one should be forced from their home because a corporate landlord wants to double the rent. Local communities — not Sacramento politicians — should decide what housing protections their residents need.

Signed by: Michael Weinstein, President, AIDS Healthcare Foundation; Members of the Coalition for Economic Survival

===== ARGUMENT AGAINST PROPOSITION 33 =====
Economists broadly agree that rent control reduces the supply of available housing and worsens housing shortages. California already has a severe housing shortage — we need to build more homes, not create policies that discourage construction.

Proposition 33 would allow cities to impose vacancy control, meaning landlords could never reset rent to market rate, even between tenancies. This would devastate property values, reduce investment in rental housing, and drive small landlords out of the market. It would make California's housing crisis worse, not better.

Signed by: Elizabeth Ashford, Senior VP, California Association of Realtors; California Chamber of Commerce

Support committees: approximately $65 million raised, with the AIDS Healthcare Foundation providing the overwhelming majority (over $46 million directly for Prop 33).

===== ELECTION RESULT =====
FAILED: 40% Yes, 60% No.
