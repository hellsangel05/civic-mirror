CALIFORNIA — NOVEMBER 5, 2024 GENERAL ELECTION
PROPOSITION 4
SAFE DRINKING WATER, WILDFIRE PREVENTION, DROUGHT PREPAREDNESS, AND CLEAN AIR BOND ACT OF 2024

===== BALLOT QUESTION =====
Shall the state authorize $10 billion in general obligation bonds to fund projects addressing climate change, including safe drinking water, wildfire prevention, drought preparedness, clean air, coastal resilience, biodiversity, extreme heat mitigation, sustainable agriculture, and parks?

===== IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS (Legislative Analyst, excerpted) =====
Proposition 4 authorizes $10 billion in general obligation bonds for a wide range of environmental and climate-related projects. The bond allocations are approximately: $3.8 billion for safe drinking water and water infrastructure (including groundwater cleanup, drought preparedness, and flood protection); $1.95 billion for wildfire prevention and extreme heat projects; $1.9 billion for natural lands, parks, and wildlife conservation; $1.2 billion for coastal lands, bays, and ocean protection; $850 million for clean energy projects; and $300 million for sustainable and resilient agriculture.

At least 40 percent of bond funds must be used for activities that directly benefit communities with lower incomes or those more vulnerable to the impacts of climate change. The bond money would be distributed by various state agencies through grants and direct expenditures.

===== FISCAL IMPACT STATEMENT =====
Increased state costs of about $400 million annually for approximately 40 years to repay the bond. Total cost to taxpayers including interest is approximately $15.6 billion. Since the state has to pay interest on the money it borrows, the total cost of the bond would be about 10 percent more (after adjusting for inflation) than if the state paid up front.

===== ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF PROPOSITION 4 =====
California faces urgent threats from wildfires, water pollution, drought, extreme heat, and sea level rise. Climate change is making all of these worse. Proposition 4 invests in proven solutions: cleaner drinking water, wildfire prevention, flood protection, and parks and open spaces that improve public health.

The measure prioritizes disadvantaged communities that are hit hardest by environmental hazards and have the fewest resources to cope. At least 40 percent of funds must directly benefit these communities. This is a responsible investment in California's future that protects families and creates good-paying jobs.

Signed by: Gavin Newsom, Governor of California; Toni G. Atkins, President pro Tempore

===== ARGUMENT AGAINST PROPOSITION 4 =====
Bonds are the most expensive way for the government to pay for things. This $10 billion in borrowing will cost taxpayers nearly $16 billion when interest is included. California should pay for environmental projects without taking on more debt.

Some of the money could go toward unproven technologies. The measure bundles too many different programs into one vote, preventing voters from prioritizing individual environmental needs. This is a fixed-price, fixed-menu meal — voters cannot pick and choose which projects deserve funding.

Signed by: Jon Coupal, President, Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association
