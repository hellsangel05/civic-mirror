LOS ANGELES COUNTY — NOVEMBER 5, 2024 GENERAL ELECTION
COUNTY MEASURE G
LOS ANGELES COUNTY GOVERNMENT STRUCTURE, ETHICS AND ACCOUNTABILITY CHARTER AMENDMENT

===== BALLOT QUESTION =====
PROPOSED COUNTY CHARTER AMENDMENT. Shall the measure amending the Los Angeles County Charter to create an elected County Executive; create an independent Ethics Commission to increase restrictions on lobbying and investigate misconduct; establish a nonpartisan Legislative Analyst to review proposed County policies; increase the Board of Supervisors from five to nine elected members; require County departments to present annual budgets in public meetings; using existing funding sources with no additional taxes to implement, as detailed in the charter amendment ordinance, be adopted?

===== IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS (County Counsel, excerpted) =====
Measure G would amend the Los Angeles County Charter — last substantially revised in 1912 — to restructure County government in several ways:

1. Create a new elected County Executive, directly accountable to voters, responsible for day-to-day administration currently handled by the appointed Chief Executive Officer.
2. Expand the Board of Supervisors from five seats to nine seats over a phased implementation, reducing the population size of each supervisorial district.
3. Establish an independent Ethics Commission with authority to investigate misconduct by County officials and employees and to enforce lobbying restrictions, including a two-year post-employment lobbying ban for former officials.
4. Establish a nonpartisan County Legislative Analyst's office to provide independent fiscal and policy analysis of proposed actions.
5. Require each County department to present its annual budget at a public meeting.

A "yes" vote is a vote to adopt these charter changes. A "no" vote leaves the existing charter in place with its five-member Board of Supervisors and appointed CEO.

===== FISCAL IMPACT STATEMENT =====
Implementation is to be funded through the existing County budget (approximately $45 billion annually) with no new taxes. One-time implementation costs are estimated at approximately $8 million or more, to be absorbed by reallocating existing resources. Ongoing costs associated with the larger Board, new Ethics Commission, and Legislative Analyst's office are to be funded within existing appropriations.

===== ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE G =====
Los Angeles County's charter hasn't had a serious update since 1912. Back then, each supervisor represented about 300,000 people. Today, each one represents two million — more than most U.S. senators. That's not accountable government; that's an unaccountable empire.

A YES vote on Measure G:
- Creates an independent Ethics Commission that can actually investigate misconduct
- Expands the Board of Supervisors to nine members so your representative is closer to you
- Adds an elected County Executive directly accountable to voters
- Bans former officials from lobbying the County for two years
- Requires department budgets to be presented in public meetings
- Uses existing funding — no new taxes

It's time to modernize a 1912 government for the 21st century.

===== ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE G =====
Measure G adds new layers of bureaucracy without solving any real problem. Creating a new elected County Executive for 10 million residents — with no term limits — risks concentrating power in a single office and creating a de facto "mayor of LA County."

The $8 million implementation cost is money that could go to homelessness, mental health, or public safety. Opposition comes from firefighters, sheriffs' deputies, and frontline labor groups who know where those funds are most urgently needed.

A NO vote keeps the current five-member Board structure in place and protects existing services from being raided to pay for a government reorganization nobody asked for.
