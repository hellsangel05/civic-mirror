LOS ANGELES COUNTY — NOVEMBER 5, 2024 GENERAL ELECTION
COUNTY MEASURE A
LOS ANGELES COUNTY HOMELESSNESS SERVICES AND AFFORDABLE HOUSING ORDINANCE

===== BALLOT QUESTION =====
To fund mental health treatment, drug and alcohol treatment, rental assistance, housing, and services to reduce homelessness, and to provide affordable housing, including for seniors, veterans, disabled people, and low-income families, shall an ordinance permanently establishing a one-half cent sales tax to provide approximately $1,076,000,000 annually, replacing the existing one-quarter cent homelessness sales tax that expires in 2027, with independent citizens' oversight and mandatory annual audits, be adopted?

===== IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS (County Counsel, excerpted) =====
Measure A would enact an ordinance imposing a permanent one-half percent (0.5%) sales and use tax throughout Los Angeles County, including within incorporated cities, to fund programs that reduce and prevent homelessness and provide affordable housing. The measure would repeal the existing one-quarter percent (0.25%) sales and use tax enacted by voters as Measure H in 2017, which is otherwise scheduled to expire in 2027.

The net effect of Measure A is to increase the existing sales and use tax rate by one-quarter percent (0.25%) and to make that increase permanent. A "yes" vote is a vote in favor of enacting the new half-percent tax and repealing the existing quarter-percent tax. A "no" vote is a vote against enacting the new tax; the existing quarter-percent tax would continue until it expires in 2027.

===== FISCAL IMPACT STATEMENT =====
Based on California Department of Tax and Fee Administration records for the period September 2023 through August 2024, the half percent (0.5%) sales and use tax is estimated to generate approximately $1.076 billion in local government revenue in the first year of collection, with collections continuing in perpetuity. Revenue is allocated as follows: 61.25% to the County for homelessness prevention, mental health treatment, substance abuse treatment, and related services; 35.75% to the Los Angeles County Affordable Housing Solutions Agency for affordable housing; and 3% to the Los Angeles County Development Authority for administrative oversight. Annual independent audits are required. The measure has no expiration date.

===== ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE A =====
Measure A takes a new and different approach to reducing homelessness. It replaces the existing Measure H with a strengthened accountability structure, mandatory independent audits, and a citizens' oversight board that reports publicly on outcomes, not just spending.

A YES vote on Measure A means:
- More permanent affordable housing — including for seniors, veterans, disabled residents, and working families
- Expanded mental health and drug treatment capacity
- Support for clearing encampments and reconnecting people to services
- Stronger neighborhood safety
- Independent oversight with the power to redirect funds that are not working

Los Angeles County's homelessness crisis demands a sustained response. Measure A gives us the stable funding to end the crisis and the accountability to make sure the money is spent well.

===== ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE A =====
Measure A doubles the sales tax that voters already passed in 2017 for homelessness — and makes it permanent. Despite spending billions on homelessness over the last decade, the crisis has gotten worse, not better. Raising the sales tax again rewards failure.

Los Angeles County's sales tax already exceeds 10% in many areas, higher than almost anywhere in the country. This measure is regressive — it falls hardest on working families and seniors on fixed incomes who can least afford it. Before raising taxes again, the County should be required to show results.

A NO vote on Measure A keeps the existing Measure H tax in place until 2027, giving the County time to prove its spending works before asking voters for more.
